## 3. What This Tells You, Practically

1. **Her strongest, most direct overlap with your angle is Section C (data stream mining/concept drift) combined with Section A (fraud/anomaly detection).** A proposal framed as "concept-drift-aware anomaly detection for transaction streams" sits squarely in territory she has published in for over a decade — this is a much stronger pitch than a generic "AI for banking" framing.
2. **Section F (privacy-preserving ML on sensitive data)** is a newer, active direction for her. If your proposal can connect fraud detection with privacy-preserving computation on transaction data (a real, current fintech concern), that touches both an established strength and where her group is currently pushing.
3. Several of her past PhD students (Tsang, Lim, Anderson, Zhang, Julian) each anchored their whole thesis in one narrow slice of this space — this is the model to follow: pick one specific, narrow technical problem, not "machine learning for banking" broadly.
4. She frequently co-supervises (with Gillian Dobbie, Albert Bifet, Patricia Riddle) — if your topic sits between two of these threads (e.g. stream mining + privacy), a co-supervision angle may be worth raising.
