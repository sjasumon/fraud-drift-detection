3. DEFINE THE RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION BEFORE FINAL EVALUATION

Prepare a focused research protocol around this candidate question:
Can a retraining policy that accounts for available labels and their delay maintain fraud recall at a fixed review workload while reducing computational cost compared with periodic retraining?

Review relevant primary literature and document the closest methods, their assumptions and the exact proposed difference. Drift adaptation and delayed-label fraud detection already have substantial prior work; do not claim novelty merely from combining standard components. A starting reference is:
https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1044896

Keep the contribution achievable and falsifiable. Prefer one well-tested adaptation policy over a collection of unrelated models. Decide the primary metric, comparison, tuning budget, evaluation periods, practical improvement or non-inferiority margin, and uncertainty method before examining the final period. Version and timestamp this protocol. Clearly distinguish a proposed contribution from an experimentally demonstrated one.

Separate transaction authenticity from feedback authenticity. If actual fraud-confirmation timestamps are absent, state that observed delayed-feedback effectiveness cannot be established from that dataset. Do not describe fixed or sampled delays as observed behavior. Under this real-output requirement, exclude simulated-delay results from the primary empirical evidence. An immediate-label or assumed-availability benchmark must be explicitly qualified and cannot substantiate claims about operational feedback delay. Keep obtaining observed confirmation timestamps as a documented research dependency where necessary.

4. IMPLEMENT A LEAKAGE-SAFE TEMPORAL PROTOCOL

Use chronological training and development periods plus an untouched future evaluation period. Define boundaries from available data duration and fraud counts, document the rationale, and freeze them before final evaluation. Use additional future windows where the data supports them. Preserve the natural evaluation class distribution.

Fit preprocessing, feature selection, hyperparameters, calibration and decision policies using permitted earlier information only. Use appropriate chronological development partitions or temporal cross-fitting; never tune on final evaluation outcomes.

Require feature-availability documentation. Historical features must use prior observable transactions, never future records or unavailable fraud labels. For tied timestamps, use a justified observed sequence or score the tied group before updating shared history. Do not infer causal ordering solely from transaction IDs.

Score each event before its label becomes available under the explicitly documented protocol. Store the original score, decision, event time, model revision, threshold, and subsequent feedback time if observed. Never substitute predictions recomputed after retraining. Unknown or pending outcomes are not legitimate-transaction labels. Account for incomplete follow-up when calculating metrics.

Verify that warmup records are not counted as future evaluation, training rows are not duplicated, missing identities are not merged into a fictitious customer, and old model generations are handled consistently. Check recent-memory behavior when available verified transactions are older than the chosen window. Preserve the same information availability for every comparator.

5. IMPROVE AND COMPARE THE METHODS FAIRLY

Compare at least static, periodic, recent-window and adaptive retraining strategies using the same initial fitting policy, event stream and label availability. Include a simple competitive baseline and a relevant established adaptive comparator identified in the literature review. Give competing approaches documented, comparable tuning and compute budgets.

Evaluate implemented feature groups through ablation: transaction features, identity information, missingness and strictly prior behavioral history. Measure the effect of calibration, threshold policy and recent-memory settings separately where relevant. Distinguish improvements due to extra information or tuning from improvements due to the adaptation policy.

Extend the current F1-based threshold policy to an operational objective such as recall at a specified review capacity or false-positive constraint. Choose that policy using past development information. Daily top-k ranking using the complete day's scores is a batch-review evaluation, not a real-time decision policy; label and test these separately.

Replace promotion based on any tiny F1 increase with a documented acceptance rule requiring sufficient evidence and acceptable quality and runtime. Avoid repeatedly treating an already inspected holdout as fresh independent evidence. Retain the active model if no candidate meets the criteria, and record rejected candidates and reasons.

Do not promise a particular accuracy, recall or percentage improvement. A valid negative or mixed result must remain visible.

Profile ingestion, history computation, preprocessing, training, prediction, database writes and reporting separately. Investigate:

- Efficient history-state updates instead of full-table Python dictionary expansion.
- Reusable feature representations and reduced copies, with temporal boundaries preserved.
- An index supporting verified-record retrieval by model family and event time.
- Efficient batch inference and durable batched persistence.
- Bounded retraining and resource isolation if background training degrades inference.

Use profiling evidence to choose changes; do not introduce distributed infrastructure without a demonstrated need. Verify prediction/decision parity for intended behavior-preserving optimizations. If an optimization changes the learned model or decision policy, reevaluate quality.

Include scoring with persistence enabled, concurrent requests and background retraining. Use real held-out transaction records for workload replay and label it as replay. Repeated records used for load generation do not become additional independent observations in accuracy evaluation.

Report cold and warm behavior, run duration and repeated measurements. Define acceptance targets before the optimization comparison using the available hardware and workload. Do not infer a production SLA from unit tests or local model-only timing.
