\begin{abstract}
Financial fraud detection operates in a highly adversarial and non-stationary environment where perpetrators continually modify tactics to circumvent deployed controls---a phenomenon formalized in stream mining as concept drift. While supervised adaptive learning algorithms are frequently proposed, existing academic literature relies on two unrealistic assumptions: (1) \textit{the immediate verification fallacy}, which assumes labels arrive instantaneously rather than with realistic 30--90 day dispute lags; and (2) \textit{entity memorization}, wherein apparent adaptive improvements stem from memorizing repeating transaction identifiers rather than learning generalizable behavioral distributions.

In this work, we conduct an empirical study of concept drift adaptation centered on the longitudinal IEEE-CIS fraud detection stream ($590{,}540$ transactions across 26 sequential weeks, with $21$ out-of-sample test weeks), while utilizing PaySim Mobile Money ($6{,}362{,}620$ transactions) as a case study in benchmark saturation and multi-seed significance testing. We enforce strict temporal lookahead isolation, evaluate per-week paired differences across the $N=21$ out-of-sample weeks, and compute moving block bootstrap confidence intervals on the paired performance delta (with block length $b=3$ weeks to preserve serial autocorrelation). Across the overall stream, the drift-adaptive model achieves a weekly recall gain of $+10.69\% \pm 6.27\%$ ($d_z = 1.70$, Benjamini-Hochberg $p_{\text{FDR}} = 5.60 \times 10^{-7}$, moving block bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+8.02\%, +14.33\%]$ under $b=3$) and an AUC-PR gain of $+0.0728 \pm 0.0311$ ($d_z = 2.34$, $p_{\text{FDR}} = 9.85 \times 10^{-9}$, MBB 95\% CI: $[+0.0630, +0.0881]$). Under direct categorical identifier ablation (raw card and address attributes excluded), the adaptive model captures $+7.21\% \pm 1.31\%$ more frauds ($76.62\% \pm 0.35\%$ vs. $69.41\% \pm 1.07\%$, $d_z = 5.49$, paired $t = 12.27, p_{\text{FDR}} = 4.23 \times 10^{-4}$, two-sided Wilcoxon $W=0.0, p = 0.0625$, bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+6.28\%, +8.29\%]$) on proxy cardholders upon their first-ever arrival in the stream. We present this as empirical evidence against naive categorical memorization, while highlighting that card-derived temporal aggregates ($C, D, V$) retain indirect entity signals and noting that seen-card recall ($48.90\% \to 61.39\%$) remains lower than unseen-card recall ($66.30\% \to 73.79\%$) due to account-takeover mimicry.

Crucially, our comparative baseline analysis reveals that sliding-window retraining ($W=4\text{w}$) achieves superior precision ($26.98\%$ vs. $23.52\%$) and F1-score ($0.3829$ vs. $0.3565$) over adaptive retraining by purging stale concepts, while error-based ADWIN/DDM detectors triggered on 17 of 21 test weeks ($22$ total retrains), behaving essentially as near-weekly retraining under acute class imbalance. Conversely, we show that single-run micro-gains on saturated streams (PaySim $F1: 0.9955 \to 0.9978$) are not statistically significant ($p = 0.2871$, bootstrap CI on delta crosses zero: $[-0.0001, +0.0015]$). In exploratory single-seed simulations, standard 8-week chargeback lags degrade out-of-sample recall from $72.70\%$ down to $63.45\%$, converging toward static baselines and highlighting the imperative for unsupervised feature-space monitoring during verification delay. Finally, we provide an open-source serving prototype demonstrating sub-$15$ms serving SLAs ($p99 = 13.38$ ms) evaluating web framework and serialization overhead.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Digital payments, account openings, and mobile remittances process billions of transactions daily under constant threat from organized financial crime. Machine learning models deployed in financial institutions operate under acute class imbalance (often $< 1\%$ fraud prevalence) and continuous non-stationarity. In data stream mining, changes in the underlying joint probability distribution $P(X, Y) = P(X)P(Y \mid X)$ over time are formalized as \textit{concept drift}~\cite{lu2018learning, gama2004learning}. Attackers adapt evasive vectors (shifting $P(Y \mid X)$), while macroeconomic trends and seasonal promotions alter consumer spending patterns (shifting $P(X)$).

Despite the abundance of academic drift detection literature~\cite{bifet2007learning, gama2004learning}, current experimental evaluations in financial fraud literature suffer from two critical vulnerabilities that hinder translational impact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Immediate Verification Assumption:} Almost all streaming benchmark papers assume ground-truth labels $y_t$ are available immediately after prediction for model retraining~\cite{dalpozzolo2015credit}. In industrial reality, fraudulent transactions are confirmed only after cardholders notice unauthorized activity, initiate chargebacks, and endure banking investigation cycles, creating a $30$ to $90$-day verification latency~\cite{carcillo2018scarff}.
    \item \textbf{Entity Memorization vs. Genuine Concept Adaptation:} When an adaptive model demonstrates superior performance over time, does it genuinely capture evolving behavioral patterns, or is it merely memorizing repeated card numbers, device tokens, and customer accounts that recur throughout the stream? Without isolating entity identifiers, reported adaptive gains may simply be trivial identity memorization.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these open questions, this paper establishes a stream mining research platform and empirical benchmark centered on the longitudinal IEEE-CIS transaction stream ($590{,}540$ transactions across 26 weeks, 21 out-of-sample test weeks):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Generalisation \& Block-Bootstrap Rigor:} We treat sequential time slices ($N=21$ weeks) as the true unit of temporal variation, evaluating per-week paired differences ($\Delta_w$) and moving block bootstrap confidence intervals on the delta ($b=3$ weeks, following K{\"u}nsch~\cite{kunsch1989jackknife}) alongside algorithmic seed stability checks ($S=5$). We prove that single-run F1 micro-gains on saturated benchmarks (PaySim $0.9955 \to 0.9978$) are not statistically significant ($p = 0.2871$), whereas adaptive recall advantages on complex multi-feature streams are verified across time ($d_z = 1.70$, $p_{\text{FDR}} = 5.60 \times 10^{-7}$, MBB 95\% CI: $[+8.02\%, +14.33\%]$).
    \item \textbf{Evidence Against Direct Entity Memorization:} We design a leak-free entity tracking methodology on IEEE-CIS data ($N=439{,}335$ out-of-sample). When explicit cardholder and address categorical identifiers are ablated from the feature space, the adaptive model achieves $+7.21\% \pm 1.31\%$ higher recall ($76.62\% \pm 0.35\%$ vs. $69.41\% \pm 1.07\%$, $d_z = 5.49$, $t = 12.27, p_{\text{FDR}} = 4.23 \times 10^{-4}$, Wilcoxon $W=0.0, p=0.0625$, bootstrap CI on delta: $[+6.28\%, +8.29\%]$) on unseen proxy cardholders upon their first arrival, providing empirical evidence against direct token memorization while highlighting the influence of card-derived temporal aggregates ($C, D, V$).
    \item \textbf{Comparative Baselines \& Retraining Cadence:} We benchmark Static (frozen), Monthly Periodic ($K=4\text{w}$), Sliding Window ($W=4\text{w}$), and Concept-Drift-Adaptive models. We report the honest finding that Sliding Window achieves superior precision ($26.98\%$ vs. $23.52\%$) and F1 ($0.3829$ vs. $0.3565$) by discarding stale concepts, while standard error-based drift detectors trigger on 17 of 21 test weeks ($22$ retrains total), effectively operating as near-weekly retraining under extreme class imbalance.
    \item \textbf{Quantifying Verification Latency Degradation:} We model 4 operational delay regimes (immediate, 4-week, 8-week, and stochastic LogNormal) in an exploratory single-seed simulation. We quantify that an 8-week chargeback lag reduces out-of-sample recall from $72.70\%$ to $63.45\%$, converging toward static baselines and motivating future unsupervised drift monitoring on $P(X)$ during feedback delays.
    \item \textbf{Explainability and Serving Artifacts:} We integrate a dual TreeSHAP $\times (1+\text{PSI})$ attribution heuristic to explain distribution shifts and release an open-source serving prototype demonstrating lock-free atomic double-buffering and sub-$15$ms serving SLAs ($p99 = 13.38$ ms) evaluating web framework and serialization overhead.
\end{itemize}


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Concept Drift in Data Streams}
Concept drift adaptation has been extensively studied in data streams~\cite{lu2018learning}. Statistical detectors typically monitor the classification error rate or underlying feature distributions. The Adaptive Windowing algorithm (ADWIN)~\cite{bifet2007learning} maintains a variable-length sliding window using Hoeffding bounds to guarantee bounded false-alarm rates without prior knowledge of drift velocity. The Drift Detection Method (DDM)~\cite{gama2004learning} monitors the binomial error distribution, establishing warning and drift levels based on sample standard deviations. In financial transaction streams, distinguishing structural concept drift from transient volatility bursts is essential to prevent false retraining alarms, a challenge formally addressed in volatility-shift detection frameworks such as SEED~\cite{huang2014detecting} and proactive volatility-modeling detectors such as ProSeed~\cite{chen2018anticipating}.

\subsection{Fraud Benchmarks and Verification Latency}
Financial fraud benchmarks differ fundamentally from standard tabular benchmarks. The IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection dataset ($590$K transactions)~\cite{ieeecis2019} spans 182 days with high feature dimensionality. The Feedzai Bank Account Fraud (BAF) suite~\cite{jesus2022turning}, accepted at NeurIPS 2022, introduced $1$M synthetic digital account openings across 8 months, reflecting temporal non-stationarity. PaySim~\cite{lopezrojas2016paysim} captures high-volume mobile money transfers.

The critical disconnect between academic research and commercial fraud systems is \textit{verification latency}~\cite{dalpozzolo2015credit}. Dal Pozzolo et al.~\cite{dalpozzolo2015credit} first formulated the streaming verification process, noting that while legitimate transactions can be considered non-fraudulent after a dispute window, positive fraud labels arrive asynchronously. Carcillo et al.~\cite{carcillo2018scarff} developed the SCARFF architecture to process delayed feedback in credit card streams. Our work expands these foundations to multi-seed statistical significance and entity memorization audits.

\subsection{Model Explainability Under Drift}
Attributing concept drift to specific features remains a major challenge. While drift detectors indicate \textit{that} a distribution shift has occurred, they do not identify \textit{which} features drove the shift. TreeSHAP~\cite{lundberg2020local} calculates exact Shapley values for tree ensembles in polynomial time. By combining local Shapley feature sensitivity with the global Population Stability Index (PSI), our framework explains distributional divergence mechanistically.

\section{Methodology \& System Formulation}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Mathematical Stream Formulation}
We model a financial stream as a sequence of transactions arriving over continuous time:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S} = \{(x_1, y_1, \tau_1), (x_2, y_2, \tau_2), \dots, (x_N, y_N, \tau_N)\}
\end{equation}
where $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the feature vector, $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ is the ground truth label ($1 = \text{fraud}$), and $\tau_t$ is the transaction timestamp. The stream is chronologically partitioned into sequential, non-overlapping time slices $S_1, S_2, \dots, S_T$. 

To ensure zero temporal lookahead leakage, all preprocessing parameters (median imputation vectors, one-hot category vocabularies, and scale weights) are fitted strictly on the baseline training partition $\mathcal{D}_{base} = \bigcup_{k=1}^{n_{base}} S_k$ (the earliest $20\%$ of slices). Subsequent slices $S_k$ ($k > n_{base}$) are transformed strictly using this baseline pipeline.

\subsection{Streaming Comparative Learning Baselines \& Experimental Controls}
To ensure rigorous, unconfounded evaluation under identical test-before-learn streaming conditions, we establish strict experimental controls:

\subsubsection{Ensemble Capacity \& Hyperparameter Invariance}
To prevent capacity discrepancies from confounding the comparison, all gradient-boosted tree models (Static, Periodic, Sliding, and Drift-Adaptive) share strictly identical architectural hyperparameters: $M = 100$ trees, maximum tree depth of $6$, learning rate $\eta = 0.08$, subsampling ratio $0.8$, and column subsampling by tree $0.8$, trained with binary logistic loss (\texttt{logloss}).

\subsubsection{Retraining Frequency and Detector Selectivity Under Imbalance}
A critical threat to validity in drift evaluation is retraining frequency confounding: an adaptive model retraining frequently may outperform a periodic baseline simply as an artifact of compute volume rather than effective drift selectivity. In our streaming benchmark, the dual ADWIN/DDM detectors triggered retraining on 17 of the 21 out-of-sample test weeks (totaling $22$ retraining events across the 26-week stream including warm-up transitions). Under severe class imbalance ($3.5\%$ fraud prevalence, \texttt{scale\_pos\_weight} $\approx 27$), minor temporal fluctuations in classification error rates repeatedly breach drift thresholds. Consequently, supervised error-based detectors exhibit low selectivity in sparse fraud streams, effectively collapsing to near-weekly retraining. Rather than demonstrating automated compute savings, the adaptive model acts as an aggressive periodic retrainer. This finding directly motivates our research into unsupervised feature-space monitoring $P(X)$, which aims to decouple drift detection from noisy minority-class error signals.

\subsubsection{Warm-Up Isolation \& Zero-Leakage Drift Monitoring}
Drift detection is strictly restricted to out-of-sample streaming slices ($S_{n_{base}+1}, \dots, S_T$). Slices $1 \dots n_{base}$ serve solely as the baseline training window. Statistical drift detectors are never updated on warm-up data where the model would predict on its own training samples, eliminating spurious in-sample detection artifacts.

\subsubsection{Comparative Learning Baselines}
We benchmark the following learning paradigms under identical GBDT capacity:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Static Baseline (Frozen):} Trained exclusively on $\mathcal{D}_{base}$ and permanently frozen across the remaining stream ($S_{n_{base}+1}, \dots, S_T$). Because it never adapts, it serves as a lower bound illustrating temporal concept decay.
    \item \textbf{Monthly Periodic Retraining ($K=4\text{w}$):} Retrained unconditionally every 4 weeks on all accumulated historical data without statistical drift awareness (5 out-of-sample retrains).
    \item \textbf{Sliding Window Retraining ($W=4\text{w}$):} Retrained every 4 weeks strictly on the most recent 4 weeks of data (5 retrains), intentionally purging older history to test memory-bounded adaptation.
    \item \textbf{Concept-Drift-Adaptive (Proposed):} Employs dual ADWIN ($\delta = 0.002$) and DDM detectors over the prediction error stream implemented via River~\cite{montiel2021river}, triggering full GBDT retraining upon confirmed drift ($22$ total retrains).
    \item \textbf{Online Incremental Stream Ensembles:} In addition to batch-retrained trees, we evaluate pure online streaming algorithms~\cite{gomes2019machine}: \textbf{Hoeffding Adaptive Tree (HAT)}~\cite{bifet2009adaptive}, \textbf{Adaptive Random Forest (ARF)}~\cite{gomes2017adaptive}, and \textbf{Streaming Random Patches (SRP)}~\cite{gomes2019streaming}, which process transactions incrementally on a sample-by-sample basis with internal drift detection and tree replacement.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Drift Detection Latency and False Alarm Evaluation}
To evaluate detection delay (Mean Time to Detect / MTTD) and false alarm resistance where true change points $\tau^*$ are unambiguously known, we benchmark ADWIN and DDM across synthetic data streams with abrupt ($p=0.05 \to 0.25$) and gradual transitions ($p=0.05 \to 0.20$ over 500 samples) across 5 random seeds (Table~\ref{tab:drift_detection_benchmark}). ADWIN achieves zero false alarms ($0.0 \pm 0.0$ per stream) and detects abrupt shifts in $79.0 \pm 20.2$ samples, whereas DDM responds slightly faster to gradual transitions ($75.0$ samples) but incurs $2.2$ false alarms per stream.

\subsection{Simulating Asynchronous Verification Latency}
In real banking, a transaction executed at time $\tau_{tx}$ is not confirmed as fraud until dispute resolution at time $\tau_{ver}$:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{ver} = \tau_{tx} + \Delta t
\end{equation}
Following the delayed-labelling streaming formulation established in the literature~\cite{dalpozzolo2015credit, gomes2017adaptive}, we model label arrival through an asynchronous priority queue $\mathcal{Q}_{delayed}$ parameterized by operational regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Immediate Feedback (0w):} $\Delta t = 0$ (Idealized theoretical baseline).
    \item \textit{Fixed Delay (4w and 8w):} $\Delta t = 28$ days or $56$ days (reflecting standard dispute resolution cycles~\cite{dalpozzolo2015credit, carcillo2018scarff}).
    \item \textit{Parametric Stochastic Delay:} $\Delta t \sim \text{LogNormal}(\mu=1.38, \sigma=0.45)$, a synthetic parametric model chosen to produce a median latency of $\exp(1.38) \approx 3.97$ weeks ($\approx 28$ days) with heavy right-tailed dispersion extending beyond 12 weeks, matching latency scales discussed in banking stream literature~\cite{carcillo2018scarff}.
\end{enumerate}
Under verification latency, when drift triggers at slice $k$, the adaptive model is trained strictly on transactions satisfying $\tau_{ver} \le \tau_{slice\_end}(k)$. Unverified transactions are quarantined from training to prevent temporal data leakage.

\noindent\textbf{Simulation Caveats and Protocol Assumptions:} We note four operational simplifications in this simulation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Symmetric Delay Assumption:} Delay is applied uniformly to both fraudulent and legitimate transactions. In banking practice, label arrival is asymmetric: legitimate transactions are typically assumed uncompromised after a liability cutoff, whereas dispute alerts arrive asynchronously.
    \item \textit{Maturity-Time Error Scoring for Drift Tracking:} When transactions mature at slice $T_{\text{mat}}$, the supervised drift monitor evaluates classification error using the \textit{active model at maturity time} ($M_{T_{\text{mat}}}$) rather than retrieving the historical inference prediction ($M_{T_{\text{arr}}}$). While this avoids redundant retraining triggers if intervening models have already adapted to the pattern, it measures retroactive diagnostic capability rather than original decision-time accuracy.
    \item \textit{Single-Seed Scope \& Absence of Delayed-Periodic Baseline:} Due to the high compute overhead of multi-regime temporal queuing, the latency audit is conducted on a single seed ($439{,}335$ transactions) without a delayed-periodic baseline. It illustrates the broad degradation curve rather than fine-grained statistical distinctions between stochastic and fixed regimes.
    \item \textit{Slice-Boundary Evaluation Discrepancy:} Immediate feedback in this audit yields $72.70\%$ recall ($21$ retrains), whereas the main streaming pipeline reports $73.58\%$ recall ($22$ retrains). This minor difference arises because the latency audit processes matured labels in discrete weekly batches at slice boundaries, whereas the main pipeline updates River drift detectors on a continuous sample-by-sample basis.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Entity Memorization Audit Formulation}
To investigate whether adaptation learns evolving behavioral patterns or relies on memorizing recurring card tokens, we construct composite proxy identity keys $\text{UID} = \text{card1}\_\text{card2}\_\text{addr1}$ across IEEE-CIS. We note critically that this UID is an empirical proxy rather than a verified bank primary account number (PAN). We compare two feature configurations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Standard Features:} Raw card and address identifiers included.
    \item \textbf{Direct Identifier Ablation:} Categorical identifiers `card1`, `card2`, `card3`, `card5`, `addr1`, and `addr2` are strictly excised from the feature matrix. We emphasize that while direct categorical identifiers are removed, the dataset's counting columns ($C_1$--$C_{14}$), timedeltas ($D_1$--$D_{15}$), and transaction aggregates ($V_1$--$V_{339}$) were originally engineered by the payment processor over card and client keys; thus, they may still carry indirect entity-correlated behavioral signals.
\end{itemize}
Transactions in out-of-sample slices are segmented into three cohorts:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cohort 1 (Seen Cards):} Transactions whose proxy UID was observed in baseline training slices ($\text{UID} \in \mathcal{U}_{base}$).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 2 (Baseline-Unseen Cards):} Transactions whose proxy UID was not observed in baseline training slices ($\text{UID} \notin \mathcal{U}_{base}$).
    \item \textbf{Cohort 3 (Strict Stream-Unseen / Zero-Memory Test):} The subset of Cohort 2 evaluated strictly upon their very first appearance in the stream ($\text{UID} \notin \mathcal{U}_{cum\_past}$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Handling Missing Metadata \& Cohort Contamination:} A critical methodological pitfall arises when transactions lack `card2` or `addr1`. Assigning a collision-free surrogate token $\text{UID} = \text{MISSING}\_\langle\text{trans\_id}\rangle$ ensures uniqueness but forces every missing-metadata transaction to be classified as ``never seen before'', contaminating Cohort 3. To prevent this distortion, our headline evaluation isolates complete cases where `card1`, `card2`, and `addr1` are strictly populated (Method A: $447{,}446$ transactions, $75.8\%$ coverage), while reporting the tokenized population (Method B) for sensitivity analysis. A true entity-disjoint split (such as GroupTimeSeriesSplit grouped by issuing bank or account ID) remains the gold standard and is proposed as core future methodology.


\subsection{Drift Attribution Heuristic Engine}
To provide actionable explainability when concept drift is flagged, we propose a multiplicative drift-attribution heuristic combining marginal TreeSHAP importance with the Population Stability Index (PSI) for feature $j$ in drifted slice $S_k$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{AttributionScore}_j = \frac{1}{|S_k|} \sum_{i \in S_k} |\phi_{ij}| \times (1 + \text{PSI}_j)
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{ij}$ is the local TreeSHAP value of feature $j$ for sample $i$, and $\text{PSI}_j$ is the Population Stability Index comparing feature $j$'s empirical distribution in $S_k$ against baseline $S_{base}$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{PSI}_j = \sum_{b=1}^{B} (p_{kb} - p_{0b}) \ln\left(\frac{p_{kb}}{p_{0b}}\right)
\end{equation}
This formulation modulates global predictive importance by the magnitude of observed distribution shift: when $\text{PSI}_j = 0$, attribution collapses to baseline TreeSHAP importance, while divergent covariates ($\text{PSI}_j > 0.25$) are amplified. This heuristic prevents benign covariate shift (high PSI, zero SHAP) and stationary predictive features (high SHAP, zero PSI) from dominating root-cause reports. To validate this heuristic without circular rank comparisons, we benchmark attribution on synthetic streams with known, ground-truth injected drift (Section~\ref{sec:attribution_eval}): true predictive drift drivers achieve $100\%$ top-2 rank identification across all seeds, while pure noise features undergoing severe benign covariate shift ($\text{PSI} > 5.0$) are correctly suppressed ($\text{SHAP} \approx 0.05$).

\subsection{Multi-Dataset Benchmark Portfolio}
Our experimental platform centers on the longitudinal IEEE-CIS fraud detection stream while evaluating PaySim Mobile Money to assess benchmark saturation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (Primary Stream):} $590{,}540$ transactions across 26 sequential weekly slices ($182$ days), $3.50\%$ fraud prevalence ($20{,}663$ frauds). Slices 0--4 serve as the 5-week warm-up baseline, leaving 21 out-of-sample test weeks ($N=439{,}335$ transactions, $16{,}660$ frauds) for unconfounded streaming evaluation.
    \item \textbf{PaySim Mobile Money (Saturation Case Study):} $6{,}362{,}620$ transactions across 5 weekly slices ($744$ hours), $0.129\%$ fraud prevalence ($8{,}213$ frauds). Evaluated to examine whether single-run drift claims hold under multi-seed statistical testing when feature engineering saturates performance.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Domain Schema Extensibility:} The ingestion engine implements schema-level temporal slicing and automated feature normalization for the Feedzai Bank Account Fraud (NeurIPS 2022) suite ($1{,}000{,}000$ applications across 8 monthly slices), serving as an extensible data adapter for future cross-domain research without requiring schema refactoring.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Significance and Longitudinal Hypothesis Testing}
Table~\ref{tab:statistical_rigor} and Fig.~\ref{fig:errorbars} present the longitudinal evaluation across non-stationary streaming slices.

\subsubsection{Distinguishing Algorithmic Seed Stability from Temporal Generalisation}
To avoid methodological pitfalls in stream mining, we distinguish between algorithmic seed sensitivity and temporal generalisation. Evaluating $K=5$ random seeds on a fixed temporal split evaluates tree initialization variance ($\sigma_{\text{seed}} < 0.4\%$), verifying that XGBoost's feature and row subsampling do not induce stochastic volatility. However, treating seed variation on fixed data as the denominator for $t$-tests would measure seed noise rather than generalisation over time, yielding artificially inflated effect sizes.

Consequently, for longitudinal stream analysis, our primary inferential statistical unit is the sequential time slice ($N=21$ out-of-sample weeks for IEEE-CIS). We evaluate per-week paired differences ($\Delta_w = M_{\text{adaptive}, w} - M_{\text{baseline}, w}$), paired two-tailed Student's $t$-tests ($df = 20$), two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and 2,000-replicate non-parametric moving block bootstrap confidence intervals on the paired performance delta $\Delta$ with block length $b=3$ weeks (following K{\"u}nsch~\cite{kunsch1989jackknife}) to preserve serial autocorrelation. Multiple comparisons are controlled using the Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) procedure~\cite{benjamini1995controlling}. For PaySim and IEEE-CIS entity cohort audits, we evaluate multi-seed initialization stability across $S=5$ random seeds on out-of-sample evaluation sets.

\subsubsection{Deconstructing Single-Run Micro-Gains on PaySim}
On PaySim, an initial single-run experiment suggested an F1 increase from $0.9955$ (Static) to $0.9978$ (Adaptive). However, multi-seed evaluation across the out-of-sample test slices (Slices 3--5) reveals that the static baseline achieves $0.9958 \pm 0.0002$ while the adaptive model yields $0.9964 \pm 0.0009$ across random seeds (paired delta $+0.0006 \pm 0.0011$). A paired two-tailed $t$-test across the $S=5$ seeds indicates $t = 1.23, p = 0.2871$ ($df=4$, $p_{\text{FDR}} = 0.2871$), the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test yields $W=3.0, p=0.6250$, and the bootstrap 95\% CI on the delta crosses zero ($[-0.0001, +0.0015]$). Because $p > 0.05$, the apparent advantage is statistically indistinguishable from noise. 

This outcome is scientifically instructive: in mobile money transfers, unauthorized cash depletion creates a direct accounting disparity ($\text{errorBalanceOrig} = \text{newbalanceOrig} + \text{amount} - \text{oldbalanceOrg}$). Once this feature is engineered, all models operate near ceiling ($F1 > 0.995$). Reporting this result underscores the danger of uncritically claiming algorithmic superiority from single-seed runs on saturated benchmarks.

\subsubsection{Empirical Evidence Against Direct Categorical Memorization on IEEE-CIS}
In contrast, on the complex IEEE-CIS stream where fraud vectors evolve across behavioral counting and timedelta dimensions ($C_1$--$C_{14}$, $D_1$--$D_4$), the adaptive advantages are robust and statistically significant across all out-of-sample weeks ($N=21$ weeks):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Across the \textbf{Overall Stream}, the adaptive model achieves a weekly recall gain of $+10.69\% \pm 6.27\%$ ($d_z = 1.70$, $t = 7.81, p = 1.68 \times 10^{-7}, p_{\text{FDR}} = 5.60 \times 10^{-7}$; two-sided Wilcoxon $W = 6.0, p = 1.34 \times 10^{-5}$; moving block bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+8.02\%, +14.33\%]$ under $b=3$). Weekly AUC-PR increases by $+0.0728 \pm 0.0311$ ($d_z = 2.34$, $t = 10.71, p_{\text{FDR}} = 9.85 \times 10^{-9}$; Wilcoxon $W = 1.0, p = 1.91 \times 10^{-6}$; MBB 95\% CI: $[+0.0630, +0.0881]$). Weekly F1-Score improves by $+0.0290 \pm 0.0174$ ($d_z = 1.66$, $t = 7.62, p_{\text{FDR}} = 6.08 \times 10^{-7}$; Wilcoxon $W = 0.0, p = 9.54 \times 10^{-7}$; MBB 95\% CI: $[+0.0210, +0.0408]$).
    \item In \textbf{Cohort 1 (Seen Cards)}, evaluated across $S=5$ random seeds on complete cases, the adaptive model captures $61.39\% \pm 0.48\%$ of frauds vs. $48.90\% \pm 0.48\%$ for static ($+12.49\% \pm 0.35\%$ gain, $t = 79.62, p_{\text{FDR}} = 2.98 \times 10^{-7}$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.0625$).
    \item In \textbf{Cohort 2 (Unseen Cards)}, the adaptive model catches $73.79\% \pm 0.56\%$ vs. $66.30\% \pm 0.79\%$ ($+7.49\% \pm 0.56\%$ gain, $t = 29.79, p_{\text{FDR}} = 1.01 \times 10^{-5}$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.0625$).
    \item In \textbf{Cohort 3 (Strict Stream-Unseen Cards / Zero-Memory Arrival)}, evaluated on clean complete cases (Method A) upon their first appearance under direct categorical identifier ablation (raw card and address tokens stripped), the adaptive model achieves $76.62\% \pm 0.35\%$ recall vs. $69.41\% \pm 1.07\%$ for static ($+7.21\% \pm 1.31\%$ net gain, paired $t = 12.27, p = 2.54 \times 10^{-4}, p_{\text{FDR}} = 4.23 \times 10^{-4}, df=4$; two-sided Wilcoxon $W = 0.0, p = 0.0625$, which is the exact mathematical lower bound for $N=5$ seeds; bootstrap 95\% CI on delta: $[+6.28\%, +8.29\%]$ and Student's $t$ 95\% CI: $[+5.59\%, +8.85\%]$) with Cohen's $d_z = 5.49$.
\end{itemize}
Because the bootstrap 95\% confidence interval on the Cohort 3 delta strictly excludes zero ($[+6.28\%, +8.29\%]$), these findings provide substantial empirical evidence that the adaptive model's performance does not depend on memorizing explicit categorical card tokens.

\noindent\textbf{Nuanced Cohort Dynamics and Methodological Limitations:} An instructive pattern emerges when examining recall levels across cohorts: seen-card fraud recall ($48.90\% \to 61.39\%$) is substantially lower than unseen-card recall ($66.30\% \to 73.79\%$ and $69.41\% \to 76.62\%$). In financial fraud streams, returning compromised cards frequently indicate account takeover (ATO), where unauthorized transactions mimic established customer behaviors and remain close to historical spending patterns. Conversely, novel cards tested in rapid automated attack bursts generate gross anomalies in transaction velocities ($C_1$--$C_{14}$) and timedeltas ($D_1$--$D_{15}$), which GBDT ensembles readily intercept.

We present this as evidence against naive categorical memorization rather than absolute mathematical proof of identifier blindness. Two caveats remain: First, while explicit card/address identifiers are removed, engineered counting ($C$), timedelta ($D$), and aggregation ($V$) columns were originally constructed by the processor over cardholder keys and retain indirect behavioral fingerprints. Second, composite proxy keys ($\text{card1}\_\text{card2}\_\text{addr1}$) only approximate genuine banking cards. A definitive gold-standard test requires issuing-bank PANs evaluated under entity-disjoint holdouts (e.g., \texttt{GroupTimeSeriesSplit}), which we outline as future doctoral research.

\subsection{Comparative Streaming Baseline Analysis \& Trade-Offs}
Table~\ref{tab:ieeecis_streaming} reports whole-stream out-of-sample metrics across Weeks 6--26 (0-indexed slices 5--25, following the 5-week warm-up) of IEEE-CIS under identical hyperparameter capacities ($M=100$ trees, depth 6, $\eta=0.08$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sliding Window Precision Superiority:} While Sliding Window ($W=4\text{w}$) achieves lower recall ($65.92\%$) than Adaptive ($73.58\%$), it achieves the \textit{highest precision} ($26.98\%$ vs. $23.52\%$) and \textit{highest F1-score} ($0.3829$ vs. $0.3565$) across all evaluated models. By intentionally purging transactions older than 4 weeks, the sliding window model eliminates stale decision boundaries, substantially reducing false positive alerts on evolving legitimate traffic.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Retraining Cadence vs. Monthly Periodic:} Against Monthly Periodic ($K=4\text{w}$, 5 retrains), Adaptive Retraining achieves $+2.24\%$ higher recall ($73.58\%$ vs. $71.34\%$, paired weekly delta $+2.19\% \pm 2.90\%$, $d_z = 0.75$, $t=3.45, p_{\text{FDR}} = 0.0036$, MBB 95\% CI: $[+1.67\%, +3.52\%]$) and $+0.0221$ higher AUC-PR ($0.5318$ vs. $0.5097$). However, the adaptive detector fired on 17 of 21 test weeks ($22$ total retrains). Because error rates fluctuate in imbalanced streams, error-based detectors lack selectivity and trigger near-weekly retraining, requiring over $4\times$ more retraining compute for modest gains.
    \item \textbf{Metric Sensitivity and Thresholding Artifacts:} At a fixed $0.5$ classification threshold with \texttt{scale\_pos\_weight} $\approx 27$, predictions are heavily biased toward recall at the expense of precision ($\approx 23\%$). When evaluated on threshold-independent AUC-PR, the performance gap between Adaptive ($0.5318$), Sliding Window ($0.5099$), and Monthly Periodic ($0.5097$) is modest ($\approx 0.022$). This demonstrates that reported gains in fraud literature are often sensitive to thresholding choices, reinforcing the necessity of reporting AUC-PR alongside fixed-budget alert curves.
    \item \textbf{Pure Streaming Ensembles (River):} Canonical incremental stream models---Hoeffding Adaptive Tree (HAT)~\cite{bifet2009adaptive}, Adaptive Random Forest (ARF)~\cite{gomes2017adaptive}, and Streaming Random Patches (SRP)~\cite{gomes2019streaming}---process transactions with zero batch retraining latency ($>12{,}000$ samples/sec on HAT). Under extreme class imbalance ($<1\%$ fraud), however, batch-retrained histogram ensembles with negative downsampling or scale weighting achieve higher overall fraud recall ($73.58\%$), highlighting an architectural trade-off between streaming throughput and minority-class recall.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decisive Multi-Seed Adaptation Benchmark: Unsupervised $P(X)$ Triggers}
To directly test whether covariate drift triggers can mitigate label delay without costly blind weekly retraining, we benchmarked four architectures across $S=5$ random seeds ($[42, 43, 44, 45, 46]$) and five verification latency horizons ($\delta \in \{0, 1, 2, 4, 8\text{ weeks}\}$), reported in Table~\ref{tab:label_delay_adaptation_frontier} and Fig.~\ref{fig:label_delay_frontier}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Latency Blindness of Supervised Monitoring:} Under immediate feedback ($\delta=0\text{w}$), supervised ADWIN/DDM achieves $81.47\% \pm 12.82\%$ recall with $7.4$ retrains. However, when dispute delay reaches $\delta=1\text{w}$ ($7$ days), recall decays to $75.94\% \pm 7.72\%$, and by $\delta=2\text{w}$ ($14$ days), it drops to $70.72\% \pm 2.95\%$. Supervised error-monitoring suffers from structural latency blindness: error counters cannot increment until chargeback disputes mature weeks after an attack surge.
    \item \textbf{Sample-Efficient Unsupervised $P(X)$ Triggers:} In contrast, monitoring covariate distribution divergence directly via Population Stability Index ($P(X)$ triggers) delivers $80.52\% \pm 3.60\%$ recall at $\delta=1\text{w}$ and $73.76\% \pm 1.07\%$ at $\delta=2\text{w}$---strictly outperforming supervised error-tracking ($70.72\%$) while requiring only \textbf{$1.2$ cumulative retrains} across the stream (an $88\%$ reduction in retrain compute compared to Periodic-1w's $10.0$ retrains and Supervised's $6.4$ retrains).
    \item \textbf{The Inevitable Latency Collapse ($\delta \ge 4\text{w}$):} When verification latency extends to $4$ or $8$ weeks ($28$--$56$ days), all retrained architectures decay asymptotically to the static baseline floor ($65.99\% \pm 1.55\%$). Even blind weekly periodic retraining ($8$--$10$ full model updates) drops to $65.79\%$, confirming that without timely dispute resolution, retraining on stale historical distributions yields zero marginal generalization advantage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Drift Attribution \& Prototype Microservice}
\label{sec:production}

\subsection{Feature Attribution Heuristic \& Empirical Validation}
\label{sec:attribution_eval}
To provide actionable operational root-cause analysis, the pipeline executes a joint TreeSHAP $\times (1+\text{PSI})$ attribution heuristic whenever ADWIN or DDM signals concept drift. In IEEE-CIS (Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution}), the primary driver identified during the Week 6 drift surge is anonymized count attribute `C13` ($\text{PSI}=0.115, \text{SHAP}=0.284$), alongside transaction value `TransactionAmt` ($\text{PSI}=0.092, \text{SHAP}=0.187$). In PaySim, the engine pinpoints the engineered balance discrepancy feature `errorBalanceOrig` ($\text{SHAP}=3.86$), confirming that the detected distribution shift is governed by unauthorized cash depletion.

To validate this heuristic against non-circular ground truth, we benchmarked it on synthetic streams where known feature shifts are injected across 5 seeds: true predictive drifted features achieve $100\%$ top-2 rank identification, whereas pure noise features undergoing benign distribution shifts ($\text{PSI} > 5.0$) remain suppressed ($\text{SHAP} < 0.06$).

\subsection{Serving-Overhead \& Real-Time Microservice Demonstration}
To demonstrate zero-downtime serving and model hot-swapping under drift alerts, we implemented a containerized FastAPI microservice prototype (`src/api.py`) featuring an atomic double-buffering architecture (`ModelDoubleBuffer`). Read scoring requests query an active in-memory booster pointer without locks, while an asynchronous background worker trains a shadow model upon concept drift triggers and atomically swaps the active pointer.

To assess the serving infrastructure overhead (HTTP routing, Pydantic JSON serialization, and double-buffer dereferencing), we evaluated $1{,}000$ sequential scoring requests via Starlette's in-process test client on the startup warmup model ($20$ trees, $7$ features):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Throughput:} $122.9$ queries per second (QPS).
    \item \textbf{Serving Latency Distribution:} Median ($p50$) = $8.06$ ms, $p95$ = $9.88$ ms, and $p99$ = $13.38$ ms.
    \item \textbf{Sub-15ms Latency SLA:} Met across all percentiles ($p99 = 13.38\text{ ms} < 15\text{ ms}$).
\end{itemize}
We explicitly document that this test benchmarks API serving plumbing and serialization overhead rather than bare-metal tree execution: standalone profiling shows pure XGBoost inference on a full 100-tree model takes only $\approx 0.24$ ms per transaction, with the remaining $\approx 7.8$ ms consumed by web framework dispatch and JSON parsing. Full-scale production deployment across external network sockets represents a distinct engineering milestone.

\section{Limitations \& Research Roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}

While our benchmark resolves critical vulnerabilities in existing literature, three open scientific questions define the future research roadmap:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unsupervised Feature-Space Drift Adaptation ($P(X)$):} Under industrial chargeback lags ($30$--$90$ days), supervised error monitors cannot trigger timely updates, while class-imbalanced error streams cause excessive alarms. Future work will investigate proactive, unsupervised covariate shift detection directly on representations of $P(X)$ to adjust decision thresholds before ground-truth labels arrive.
    \item \textbf{Disentangling Entity Drift from Macroeconomic Shift:} Our entity audit relies on a synthetic proxy key ($\text{card1}\_\text{card2}\_\text{addr1}$) and categorical ablation. While effective against direct token memorization, derived aggregation columns ($C, D, V$) carry residual behavioral history. A rigorous isolation requires true issuing-bank primary account numbers evaluated under entity-disjoint temporal splits (\texttt{GroupTimeSeriesSplit}), enabling separate tracking of customer-level behavioral drift and macro-level attack vectors.
    \item \textbf{Privacy-Preserving Cross-Institutional Collaboration:} Financial institutions are legally constrained by data protection mandates (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) from pooling raw transaction data. Naive ensembling leaks tree split thresholds and degrades cross-bank precision. Extending this framework into secure federated tree boosting with differential privacy and secure multi-party computation represents an essential translational direction~\cite{wang2024gtree}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper provides an empirical study of concept drift adaptation in streaming financial fraud detection centered on the longitudinal 26-week IEEE-CIS benchmark ($590{,}540$ transactions), alongside PaySim Mobile Money ($6{,}362{,}620$ transactions) as a case study in benchmark saturation. Through longitudinal weekly paired evaluations and moving block bootstrap confidence intervals ($b=3$ weeks), we provide empirical evidence against naive categorical memorization, demonstrating a $+7.21\% \pm 1.31\%$ recall gain ($76.62\% \pm 0.35\%$ vs. $69.41\% \pm 1.07\%$, $d_z = 5.49$, $t = 12.27, p_{\text{FDR}} = 4.23 \times 10^{-4}$, Wilcoxon $W=0.0, p=0.0625$, bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+6.28\%, +8.29\%]$) on stream-unseen proxy cardholders under explicit identifier ablation, alongside a $+10.69\% \pm 6.27\%$ weekly recall gain across the overall stream ($d_z = 1.70, p_{\text{FDR}} = 5.60 \times 10^{-7}$, moving block bootstrap 95\% CI: $[+8.02\%, +14.33\%]$). Concurrently, we highlight that sliding-window retraining achieves superior precision ($26.98\%$ vs. $23.52\%$) and F1 ($0.3829$ vs. $0.3565$), and that card-derived behavioral aggregates ($C, D, V$) retain indirect entity signals. Conversely, we demonstrate that single-seed gains on saturated streams (PaySim $0.9955 \to 0.9978$) are not statistically significant ($t=1.23, p = 0.2871$), establishing the necessity of multi-seed hypothesis testing in stream mining. Finally, we quantify how real-world verification latency degrades supervised adaptation toward static baselines, establishing a clear empirical rationale for developing unsupervised drift monitoring during dispute delays.
